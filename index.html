A prediction model, measured against the line that already knows.

Five seasons of NFL play-by-play, a logistic-regression / gradient-boosting / random-forest ensemble, and a betting engine that de-vigs the market before it claims an edge. Everything is benchmarked against one number: the closing spread.

It does not beat the closing line. No configuration cleared the benchmark, and the edge filter built on top of it lost money on the 2025 holdout. That finding is the result — this page reports it rather than burying it under picks.

The honest part

What happened when it did bet

…

The cause is diagnosed, not guessed: the model's probabilities are flatter than the market's. It says 22% where reality is 15%, and 78% where reality is 87%. A one-sided edge filter reads that flatness as opportunity on every heavy underdog. Calibration does not fix it — isotonic recalibration flips the picks to favourites and loses more.

Pipeline

How a pick is made

01

Features, strictly lagged

Every in-game statistic enters only as a rolling average of earlier games. Four independent guards assert this, and they have caught real bugs.

02

Bagged prediction with an interval

500 block-bootstrap resamples over season-week blocks give each game a probability and a 95% interval — roughly 0.16 wide.

03

De-vig the market

Shin's method removes the book's 3.7% margin. Comparing against a raw implied probability would invent edge on every bet.

04

Require the interval to clear it

One-sided: the whole interval must sit above the fair price. A point estimate beating the market is never enough.

Walk-forward cross-validation, time-based split, 2025 held out and touched once. No random k-fold anywhere.
